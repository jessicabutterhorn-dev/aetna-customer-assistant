# Frequently Asked Questions - Missouri Aetna Plans

## ⚠️ Important: 34 Different Plans

Missouri has **34 different Aetna Medicare plans** (not just 6 types). Each plan code:
- Serves different Missouri counties
- May have different premiums, copays, and benefits
- Must be individually verified for your area

**Before enrolling, confirm your specific plan code covers your county.**

---

## Enrollment Questions

**Q: When can I enroll in an Aetna Medicare plan?**
A: During Open Enrollment (October 15 - December 7) or if you have a Special Enrollment Period due to a qualifying life event. Coverage begins January 1. First-time Medicare enrollees have a 3-month Initial Enrollment Period around their 65th birthday.

**Q: Can I change plans during the year?**
A: Only during Open Enrollment (Oct 15 - Dec 7) unless you have a qualifying life event (job loss, death in family, moving, etc.). Qualifying events allow 60-day Special Enrollment Period.

**Q: Do I lose my old coverage if I enroll in Aetna Medicare?**
A: If you had an employer plan, you'll lose it when Aetna coverage begins (January 1). If you had Medigap, it automatically ends. You cannot have both Medicare Advantage and Medigap at the same time.

**Q: Is there a penalty for late enrollment?**
A: Yes. If you don't enroll when first eligible, you may pay a permanent late enrollment penalty when you do enroll. This is added to your monthly premium.

## Coverage & Provider Questions

**Q: Do I need a referral to see a specialist?**
A: No. Aetna Medicare plans do NOT require referrals from your PCP to see specialists. However, some providers may request a treatment plan or recommendation from your doctor.

**Q: What if I want to use an out-of-network doctor?**
A: HMO plans generally do NOT cover out-of-network care (except emergencies). You'll likely pay the full cost. Some HMO-POS plans allow limited out-of-network care, but you pay more. Check your plan details.

**Q: Do I have to change doctors?**
A: Not necessarily. Your current doctor may be in-network. Contact Aetna at 1-833-570-6670 with your doctor's name to check if they're in-network. If they're not in-network, you can appeal or switch plans during Open Enrollment.

**Q: How do I find an in-network doctor?**
A: Visit AetnaMedicare.com and use the provider search tool, or call 1-833-570-6670. You can also ask your doctor's office if they accept Aetna Medicare.

**Q: Can I go to the ER without approval?**
A: Yes. Emergency care does NOT require prior authorization. Just go to the nearest ER or call 911. You'll pay the emergency copay ($150 for most plans). You only pay once per ER visit, not for each service.

## Cost Questions

**Q: What is the Maximum Out-of-Pocket (MOOP)?**
A: It's the most you'll pay in copays/coinsurance for in-network medical services in a year. For Aetna Medicare Premier (H2663-021), it's $2,900. Once reached, the plan pays 100% of covered services for the rest of the year.

**Q: Does my premium count toward my MOOP?**
A: No. Your monthly plan premium does NOT count toward your maximum out-of-pocket limit. Only copays and coinsurance count.

**Q: Do prescription drug costs count toward my MOOP?**
A: No. Part D prescription drug costs do NOT count toward your medical MOOP. They have a separate $2,100 out-of-pocket threshold for drugs.

**Q: Why does my copay vary?**
A: Different services have different copays. PCP visits might be $0, specialists $20, and hospital stays much more. Check your plan materials for specific copays.

**Q: Will my copays increase during the year?**
A: No, copays stay the same throughout the year. But copays can change on January 1 each year.

## Prescription Drug Questions

**Q: Which pharmacies can I use?**
A: You can use any Aetna pharmacy, mail-order pharmacy, or network pharmacy. Find them at AetnaMedicare.com or call 1-833-570-6670. Mail-order is usually cheaper for maintenance medications.

**Q: Can I get a 90-day supply of medication?**
A: Yes, for most drugs (Tier 1-4). Tier 5 (specialty) drugs are limited to 30-day supplies. Check with your pharmacy about mail-order 90-day options.

**Q: What is the drug deductible?**
A: $615 (2026). It applies ONLY to Tiers 3, 4, and 5. Tiers 1 and 2 drugs, insulins, and vaccines don't count toward the deductible.

**Q: My doctor prescribed brand-name, but the copay is very high. What should I do?**
A: Ask your doctor about generic alternatives. Generics are usually Tier 1 or 2 (much cheaper). If brand-name is medically necessary, your doctor can submit a "prior authorization" request for coverage.

**Q: Why does my plan require prior authorization for a drug?**
A: Prior authorization ensures the medication is medically necessary before the plan pays. It prevents unnecessary expensive drugs. Your doctor's office usually handles this — no action needed from you.

**Q: What if my medication isn't on the formulary?**
A: Contact your doctor and pharmacist. They can request "formulary exception" for Aetna to cover it, or switch to a similar medication that IS covered.

**Q: Can I get insulin for only $35?**
A: Yes! Part D insulins are capped at $35/month maximum for 30-day supply, regardless of cost-sharing tier or phase. This is a federal benefit.

## Coverage Questions

**Q: Is preventive care really free?**
A: Yes! Many preventive services are $0 copay, including annual physicals, cancer screenings, vaccines, and preventive tests. Some preventive services may have charges if not considered "routine."

**Q: What's the difference between a copay and coinsurance?**
A: **Copay** = flat fee (e.g., $20 for a doctor visit). **Coinsurance** = percentage of cost (e.g., 20% of a hospital bill). You pay the percentage after deductible.

**Q: Does the plan cover dental work?**
A: It depends on your specific plan. Most HMO-POS plans include dental with a $4,000/year benefit limit. Preventive care is free; comprehensive services require coinsurance.

**Q: Is hearing aid replacement covered?**
A: Yes, up to $1,250 per ear per year. If you need replacement, Aetna covers the benefit amount; you pay anything above that.

**Q: Is vision care covered?**
A: Yes. Annual eye exam is free, and you get a $475/year benefit for eyeglasses or contacts (at EyeMed network providers only).

## Billing & Payment Questions

**Q: How do I pay my monthly premium?**
A: Your premium is usually deducted from Social Security or paid by check. Aetna will inform you of payment method options when you enroll.

**Q: Do I still need to pay Medicare Part B?**
A: Yes, always! Your Part B premium is separate and is usually deducted from Social Security. This is in ADDITION to your Aetna plan premium.

**Q: Why did I get a bill from a provider?**
A: This could be:
- An out-of-network provider (not covered)
- A service not covered by your plan
- A billing mistake

Contact Aetna at 1-833-570-6670. Ask for an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) to understand the charge.

**Q: What's an Explanation of Benefits (EOB)?**
A: A statement showing what services were billed, what Aetna paid, and what you owe. You receive EOBs in the mail or online. Use EOBs to verify charges.

**Q: I received an unexpected bill. What should I do?**
A: Contact Aetna with the bill. It might be:
- Out-of-network (you're responsible)
- Prior auth wasn't obtained (you may not owe)
- Billing error (can be corrected)

## Plan Change Questions

**Q: Can I switch to a different Aetna plan?**
A: Yes, during Open Enrollment (Oct 15 - Dec 7). You can also switch if you have a Special Enrollment Period due to a qualifying life event.

**Q: Can I go back to Original Medicare?**
A: Yes, during Open Enrollment. You have the right to disenroll and return to Original Medicare with no penalties (as long as you have Part B).

**Q: Can I add Medigap (supplemental coverage)?**
A: Only after you disenroll from Aetna and go back to Original Medicare. You cannot have both Aetna Medicare Advantage and Medigap at the same time.

**Q: What if my doctor leaves the network?**
A: Contact Aetna immediately. You have the right to switch plans or complete a current course of treatment with the out-of-network doctor. Aetna may cover limited out-of-network care in this situation.

## Service Area & Coverage Questions

**Q: Will my plan cover me if I travel?**
A: **Within U.S.**: Yes, HMO plans cover in-network care nationwide. Out-of-network coverage is limited.
**Outside U.S.**: Emergency care only, up to $250,000 maximum coverage.
**Planned care outside U.S.**: Not covered.

**Q: What if I move to a different state?**
A: Your Missouri plan will NOT cover you outside the service area. During Open Enrollment, you can disenroll from Aetna and enroll in a plan where you're moving to. Qualify for Special Enrollment Period.

**Q: What if I move within Missouri but to a different county?**
A: Check if your new county is in your plan's service area. If not, you can disenroll during Open Enrollment or request Special Enrollment Period for the move.

## Medical Necessity & Appeals

**Q: What does "medically necessary" mean?**
A: Services that are appropriate and necessary to diagnose, treat, or manage your condition based on clinical evidence and best medical practices. Aetna determines if services are medically necessary.

**Q: What if Aetna denies coverage?**
A: You have the right to appeal. Request an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) with denial reason. File an appeal within 60 days. Your doctor can support your appeal with medical documentation.

**Q: How do I appeal a denied claim?**
A: Contact Aetna Member Services (1-833-570-6670) and request an appeal. Submit medical information supporting why the service should be covered. Aetna will respond within 30-60 days.

**Q: Can I get emergency care without worrying about approval?**
A: Yes! Emergency services do NOT require prior authorization. Go immediately to the ER or call 911. You'll pay the copay. Aetna reviews afterward to confirm it was an emergency.

## Drug Coverage & Formulary Questions

**Q: What is a formulary?**
A: A list of medications the plan covers. Aetna's formulary is "B2" (2026). Find it at AetnaMedicare.com or get a copy from your pharmacy.

**Q: What if my medication isn't on the formulary?**
A: Ask your doctor to request a "formulary exception." If approved, the plan will cover it. If denied, ask about generic alternatives or similar medications on the formulary.

**Q: What are drug tiers?**
A: 
- **Tier 1**: Preferred generics (lowest cost)
- **Tier 2**: Generics (low cost)
- **Tier 3**: Preferred brands (higher cost, 24% coinsurance)
- **Tier 4**: Non-preferred drugs (higher cost, 25% coinsurance)
- **Tier 5**: Specialty drugs (highest cost, 25% coinsurance)

**Q: What's the difference between preferred and non-preferred?**
A: Preferred drugs are on the plan's preferred list (lower cost). Non-preferred drugs are not on the preferred list (higher copay). Ask your doctor if a preferred alternative exists.

## General Aetna Medicare Questions

**Q: Is Aetna a good plan?**
A: Aetna Medicare offers plans with $0 premiums, good coverage, and extras like dental/vision/hearing. Compare benefits with other plans during Open Enrollment to see if it fits your needs.

**Q: Are Aetna plans HMO or PPO?**
A: Most Missouri plans are HMO-POS (HMO with some Point of Service flexibility). They require you to use in-network providers and have a PCP. They're not PPOs.

**Q: Do I need to worry about losing coverage?**
A: No. Medicare Advantage plans are guaranteed renewable. As long as you pay your premium and meet Medicare eligibility, Aetna cannot cancel you.
